Methodology

Data

  • UNRATE, CPIAUCSL, FEDFUNDS, UMCSENT: monthly, averaged to quarters. CPI converted to year-over-year inflation.
  • DRCCLACBS: quarterly, from 1991 Q1 (its first observation).
  • FRED is missing October 2025 for CPI and unemployment. Those quarters average the two available months.

Features and target

  • Features at quarter t: the four indicators, 4-quarter changes in unemployment and fed funds, and the current delinquency rate.
  • Target: delinquency rate at quarter t+1.

Models

  • Linear regression and ridge regression (α = 5) on standardized features.
  • Random forest: 400 trees, max depth 4, min 3 samples per leaf.
  • Metrics are on the test period. Forecasts come from each model refit on all history.

Recession Risk Signal

How closely current U.S. economic conditions resemble months that NBER later dated as recessions. Three baseline classifiers score each month from six leading indicators.

!
Early-warning signal, not an official recession call. The models learn from USREC, the NBER recession indicator. NBER's Business Cycle Dating Committee sets those dates by judgment, looking at many indicators, and typically announces them 6 to 18 months after a recession starts. A high reading means today's conditions look like past recession months. It does not mean a recession has started or will start.

Recession probability over time

Gray bands are NBER-dated recessions. Lines show each model's probability that a month's conditions resemble a recession month.

Probabilities before the test period are in-sample: the models saw those months during training. From 2005 onward they are out-of-sample. A month is classified as recession-like when its probability is 50% or higher. Most false alarms fall in 2022–25, when the yield curve was deeply inverted and sentiment hit record lows, but no recession followed.

Methodology

Data

  • USREC (target), T10Y2Y, UNRATE, UMCSENT, INDPRO and ICSA from FRED, monthly. Daily spread and weekly claims are averaged to months.
  • The window starts in January 1978, when consumer sentiment becomes monthly.

Features

  • Yield spread, and its lowest level over the past 12 months. Inversions tend to come before recessions.
  • Unemployment rise: 3-month average minus its low over the prior 12 months, similar to the Sahm rule.
  • Consumer sentiment, and year-over-year change in industrial production and initial claims.

Models and evaluation

  • Logistic regression (balanced class weights) and k-nearest neighbors (k = 15, distance-weighted) on standardized features. Random forest: 400 trees, depth 4, balanced class weights.
  • Current readings come from each model refit on all labelled history.
